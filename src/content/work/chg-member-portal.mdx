The member portal is where Medi-Cal members go to see their ID card, change their doctor and get help. The old portal was built years ago in ASP.NET WebForms. It works, but it reads like a back office: small text, jargon and buried tasks.

I redesigned it end to end, from research of the old system to Figma screens, a design system, bilingual copy, an HTML demo and a native iOS prototype.

## Who it's for

Many members are older adults, Spanish speakers, or are using the portal on a phone at a clinic's front desk. They come with one job: show my card, change my doctor, call someone. So every design decision was measured against "can someone do this in a hurry, in their language, with tired eyes?"

## Principles

- **Plain language.** About a 6th-grade reading level. "Your main doctor for checkups and when you feel sick," not "Primary Care Physician assignment."
- **English and Spanish as equals.** Every string exists in both, kept in parity by the build. Spanish uses *usted* and gender-neutral phrasing.
- **Accessible by default, not by audit.** 18px body text, 44px minimum touch targets, 2px input borders, AA contrast checked automatically, and color is never the only signal.
- **One brand color.** The plan's primary purple is the only color in the UI. Everything else comes from a neutral Bootstrap-based kit.

### Home answers "what do you need today?"

The ID card, your doctor, and four big task tiles. No dashboard of numbers. The card is on Home because showing it at the front desk is the most common reason people open the portal.

### Change my doctor in three steps

Who needs a new doctor → pick from suggestions → review and confirm. Suggestions are near your home, speak your language, and take new patients. The old rule of one change per month is shown up front, with the exact date you can change again, instead of failing at the end.

### An ID card you can actually use

"Show at front desk" opens the card full screen. "Lost your card?" explains that you can still get care. A child whose card is still being made gets their member number right away, instead of an error.

## A design system that ships

Tokens live in one JSON file. A script turns them into CSS, SCSS and Swift, and checks every text and background pairing against WCAG AA. The build fails if a color change breaks contrast.

The same tokens power a **white-label** setup: a second health plan, Community Health Plan of Imperial Valley, uses the same screens with its own color and logo. In Figma it's one variable mode; in the demo it's one dropdown.

## From Figma to code

I built the front end myself in Angular with the MDB (Bootstrap 5) UI kit, straight from the approved Figma prototype. It covers sign in, create an account, Home, the ID card with a print sheet, the four-step Change my doctor flow, contact info, password, member incentives, alerts and health videos. Brand colors are CSS variables, so the CHG/CHPIV switch is one setting, not a second codebase. All data is mock; there is no backend yet.

I also explored a native **iOS app** in SwiftUI. It follows Apple's conventions (tab bar, Dynamic Type, SF Symbols) while keeping the plan's color and the same accessibility rules.
